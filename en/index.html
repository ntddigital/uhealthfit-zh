Why Chinese herbalism

An old tradition, held to modern standards

You don't need to adopt a whole philosophy to benefit from what Chinese herbalism does well: combining plants thoughtfully, for steady everyday support. Here's what we take from it.

01

Formulas, not single ingredients

The tradition rarely relies on one hero ingredient. Classical formulas give each herb a role — a lead herb, supporting herbs, and herbs that balance the blend — the way a good recipe works. Every WiseQuest product starts from that logic.

02

Tradition, checked by modern standards

Traditional use tells us where to start. Modern manufacturing tells us what's actually in the bottle: cGMP facilities, third-party purity testing, and complete ingredient lists you can look up.

03

Built for everyday, not emergencies

Eye drops, coffee and melatonin all have their place in the moment. Our formulas are designed for the long middle: steady, daily support you can fold into a routine.†

Herbs you may already know

Less exotic than you might think

Plenty of the botanicals in our formulas are already in American kitchens, cafés and skincare aisles. Here are six of them, and where you'll find them in the WiseQuest line.

Goji berry

A tonic staple of Chinese kitchens — and one of the richest food sources of zeaxanthin, the eye nutrient you may know from eye vitamins.

Chrysanthemum

The flower in the pale-gold tea served across Asia, brewed for centuries for tired eyes and “internal heat”.

Found in LumiView →

American ginseng

Native to the forests of North America and exported to China since the 1700s, where it's valued as the gentler, “cooler” ginseng.

Found in EnerBalance →

Centella (cica)

The “cica” of K-beauty — a calming botanical used in traditional skincare long before it reached Sephora shelves.

Schisandra

The “five-flavor berry” — sweet, sour, salty, bitter and pungent at once — and a classic adaptogen.

Poria

A fungus that grows on pine roots, found in many classical formulas as the herb that keeps tonic blends balanced.
